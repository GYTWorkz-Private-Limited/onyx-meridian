# Meridian — LinkedIn Post

## Primary post (build-in-public, medium length)

Most companies are adopting AI one chatbot at a time. That doesn't scale — and it definitely doesn't govern.

Over the past few weeks I've been building **Meridian**, an AI Workforce Control Plane. The idea is simple: treat AI agents the way you treat people in an org.

You hire them into departments. You give them a role, a scope, and the tools they need. You watch what they do, approve what matters, and control what they cost.

A few of the concepts I'm most excited about:

🔹 **An org chart for your AI workforce** — a live canvas of every agent, which department it reports to, and what it's working on.

🔹 **Units of Work** — atomic, governed capabilities an agent can invoke against real systems (an ERP action, a document signature, a ledger update), instead of unbounded "do anything" access.

🔹 **Governance by default** — secrets in a vault, role-based access (Member / Dept Head / Admin), and human approvals wired into the flow.

🔹 **Cost & effectiveness control** — model choice and reasoning effort are the real cost levers, so leaders can see and steer spend per department.

The thesis: the winners in enterprise AI won't be the ones with the most agents — they'll be the ones who can **operate and govern** them like a real workforce.

Still early, but it's coming together. Would love to hear how your team is thinking about managing AI agents at scale. 👇

## Thought-leadership variant

In 2026, the constraint on enterprise AI isn't capability. It's control.

Any team can spin up an agent. Very few can answer the questions that actually matter to a business:

— Who does this agent report to?
— What systems is it allowed to touch, and under whose authority?
— Who approved that action?
— What did it cost, and was it worth it?

That's the gap I'm building **Meridian** to close — an AI Workforce Control Plane that treats agents like a real workforce: hired into departments, scoped to specific units of work, governed with role-based access and human approvals, and measured on cost and effectiveness.

The winners in enterprise AI won't be the ones with the most agents. They'll be the ones who can operate and govern them.
